Saturn's rings

Saturn's rings are made of countless pieces of water ice, from grains of dust to chunks the size of a house. They span hundreds of thousands of kilometres but are mostly only tens of metres thick.

Seen through a small telescope, the rings are visible at around 25x magnification. Every 13 to 15 years the rings line up edge-on with Earth and almost disappear from view.

Measurements from the Cassini spacecraft suggest the rings may be young in astronomical terms, perhaps a few hundred million years old, and that they are slowly raining down onto the planet.
